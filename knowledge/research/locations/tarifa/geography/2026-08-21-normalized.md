# Tarifa geography & orientation — normalized dossier

## Evidence result

Tarifa’s verified geography is unusually rich and can support a strong authority page:

- the municipality covers 419.93 km² and has eight statistical population centres;
- IGN supplies precise coordinates for Punta de Tarifa;
- Isla de Tarifa was connected to the town by a causeway in 1808;
- local official orientation places Playa Chica on the Mediterranean/Strait side and Los Lances–Valdevaqueros westward on the Atlantic side;
- MITECO describes the Strait as a water-exchange and transition zone, roughly 50 km long and narrowing to about 14 km in its central/eastern sections;
- the minimum Europe–Africa separation in the Strait zone is approximately 14.4 km;
- the municipal emergency plan classifies about 33.5 km of coast as Atlantic and 10.5 km as Mediterranean;
- the Playa de los Lances protected area covers 226 ha, and its official trail begins at Tarifa’s north-west edge;
- a municipal record places a named La Marina site at Avenida de Los Lances and Calle Mar Menor.

## Southernmost wording

The user’s intended point is valuable, but the exact wording matters. The normalized formulation is: Tarifa is Europe’s southernmost mainland city, with Punta de Tarifa on the causeway-linked island marking its southern edge.

The shorter statement “Tarifa is the southernmost point of Europe” is rejected as unqualified. Official Greek tourism identifies Gavdos as Europe’s southernmost island/tip, so “mainland city” and the island/causeway nuance must remain visible.

## Physical and practical boundaries

The causeway is a strong local Atlantic–Mediterranean orientation device, but MITECO describes dynamic exchange between water masses rather than a permanently visible seam.

The 14.4 km figure is a regional minimum continental separation, not a distance from Tarifa town, an accommodation or Tangier. It also does not prove that Africa is visible from every location.

La Marina’s place-name relationship is partially established, but AMARA Family & Surf’s entrance, walking route, view and wind exposure are not. Broad Gemini claims that La Marina is always exposed, the old town always sheltered or a named wind makes a beach categorically usable or unusable were rejected.

## Next evidence

Priority work is an exact AMARA entrance and route inspection, a municipal/GIS definition of La Marina, location-specific view observations and evidence-based wind-exposure analysis. The hydrographic boundary wording can be strengthened with an IHO or Spanish hydrographic chart.
